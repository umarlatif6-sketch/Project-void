§01 Executive Summary — Grown Not Smelted

The Cumbrian Radiotrophic Hub is PROJECT VOID's GriDul research and deployment site, sited within the Sellafield SiX Framework zone in Cumbria, England. It represents the convergence of three sovereign infrastructure principles: Grown-not-Smelted hardware fabrication using the RMW-01 Bio-Steel protocol; Radiotrophic Energy Harvesting using melanin-rich Ganoderma lucidum mycelium in proximity to licensed low-level radiation environments; and Net-Zero Sovereignty via hemp hurd substrate and closed-loop fungal cultivation.

The Sellafield SiX Framework provides the regulatory and physical co-location environment: SiX sites are licensed for novel technology deployment adjacent to nuclear facilities, with specific provisions for biological and advanced materials research. The Cumbrian Hub operates under SiX as a non-power, non-safety-critical research installation — enabling radiotrophic activation experiments under licensed supervision without requiring a nuclear site licence.

The commercial pathway targets a 4,000-unit OEM rollout of BMG Composite Nodes manufactured at the Cumbrian Hub, leveraging the site's access to controlled radiation flux for melanin pre-activation, Ganoderma substrate cultivation, and full RMW-01 QC validation. The Hub functions simultaneously as production facility, research site, and GriDul mesh anchor node.

§02 Mechanical Stability — ISO 1940-1 Compliance

All rotating components in the Cumbrian Hub installation — specifically the kinetic flywheel and tungsten WHA (Wolfram Heavy Alloy) rotors used in the mechanical proof-of-work layer — must comply with ISO 1940-1, the international standard for mechanical balance quality requirements for rotors in constant (rigid) and flexible rotor state.

The hub operates rotors at sustained speeds above 10,000 RPM for kinetic energy storage and Qi-mapping calibration. At these speeds, ISO 1940-1 Grade G2.5 balance quality is required — the tightest grade specified for precision machinery. Tungsten WHA (95% W, 3.5% Ni, 1.5% Fe) is the approved rotor material: its density (18.5 g/cm³) and thermal stability minimise dynamic imbalance across the full operating temperature range (−20°C to +85°C ambient).

Mycelium-silk passive dampening mounts are fitted at each rotor bearing point. These are composite pads of compressed Ganoderma lucidum mycelium mat and degummed Bombyx mori silk, which exhibit visco-elastic damping coefficients (loss factor η = 0.15–0.28) suitable for attenuating the 10,000+ RPM rotor's primary imbalance harmonics without steel spring mounts. This eliminates metallic resonance contamination of the acoustic measurement environment.

§03 EMC Shielding — BS EN 61000 / 42T Flux

The Cumbrian Hub's electromagnetic compatibility (EMC) shielding architecture is designed to meet BS EN 61000 (the British adaptation of IEC 61000), specifically Parts 4-3 (radiated immunity) and 4-8 (power frequency magnetic field immunity), while also providing a shielded measurement environment for the radiotrophic mycelium research programme.

The primary shielding layer is a silver-silk weave composite: degummed Bombyx mori silk woven with 0.5 mm silver wire at 40% co-weave density, forming a flexible, biocompatible Faraday-analogous barrier rated to 42T flux attenuation across the 10 MHz to 6 GHz frequency range. Unlike conventional copper mesh, the silver-silk weave maintains its shielding effectiveness under the mechanical flexing inherent to the Hub's grown-material construction.

A secondary layer of 0.5 mm solid silver EMF mesh (silver content ≥99.9%) is bonded to the interior surface of the Hub's outer wall at critical measurement zones. This provides additional magnetic field attenuation at low frequencies (50 Hz to 1 MHz), where the silver-silk weave alone is insufficient due to skin depth limitations.

§04 Net-Zero Sovereignty — Hemp Hurd & Ganoderma Cultivation

The Cumbrian Hub operates on a net-zero biological materials budget. All fungal substrate is grown on-site from hemp hurd (Cannabis sativa L. industrial variety), the woody core fibre separated from the bast during hemp processing. Hemp hurd is the optimal substrate for Ganoderma lucidum cultivation: its lignin-to-cellulose ratio (45:35) matches the enzymatic specialisation of Ganoderma's ligninolytic enzyme suite, supporting maximum mycelial colonisation density and melanin content per kilogram of substrate.

Each cultivation cycle consumes 1,000 kg of hemp hurd, producing approximately 180 kg of melanin-rich Ganoderma lucidum mycelium mass (ATCC 32472/76532) suitable for MRB-4000 substrate incorporation and BMG Node skin fabrication. The spent hemp hurd substrate post-cultivation is enriched with fungal biomass residues and is composted on-site as a carbon-sequestering amendment, completing the circular materials loop.

Net-zero energy is maintained through the kinetic flywheel PEACE system — the same kinetic proof-of-work architecture deployed in the Sovereign Node, scaled to Hub level — and radiotrophic supplemental charging via the melanin-rich mycelium skin of the building shell itself, which faces the site's licensed low-level radiation gradient.

Spent hemp hurd substrate, post-cultivation, retains approximately 35% of its original carbon content as fungal-derived biomass amendments (glucans, melanin fragments, chitin). On-site composting at ambient temperature (20–28°C) converts this to stable humic material within 8–12 weeks, sequestering approximately 120 kg CO₂-equivalent per cultivation cycle. Net carbon balance for the Hub across a 12-month manufacturing year: negative (carbon sink), factoring in solar-supplemented heating of cultivation chambers and PEACE kinetic power for processing equipment.
