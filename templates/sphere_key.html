02 Making the Sphere Key
1
Mix the solution

Combine UV-curable resin with a small amount of surfactant — approximately 20 parts resin to 1 part washing-up liquid. Stir slowly to avoid introducing large bubbles. The surfactant lowers surface tension so the bubble forms and holds.

Too much surfactant weakens the cure. Start at 1:20 and adjust.

2
Set the frequency

Play the VoidEcho-encoded audio file through the speaker — the same audio that contains the document you want to protect. This is important: the bubble is inflated by the carrier frequency of the encoded audio. At 432 Hz the Faraday pattern on the membrane will be specific to that frequency.

If you use a different frequency, the sphere key will not match a 432 Hz encoding. The frequency used to blow the bubble must match the frequency used to encode.

3
Blow the bubble

Dip the silicone tube end in the resin solution. The speaker's airflow will begin inflating the bubble as it plays. The membrane will start vibrating and taking on the Faraday geometry determined by the frequency. Allow the bubble to reach approximately 8–12 cm diameter.

4
Cure at peak vibration

While the bubble is actively vibrating — during the loudest, most stable moment of the audio — apply the UV torch to the entire surface for 30–60 seconds. Work quickly and evenly. The resin will harden with the Faraday pattern locked into the geometry.

This is the critical moment. The UV torch freezes the frequency pattern. The resulting sphere is your key.

5
Photograph the sphere

Place the cured sphere on the black matte backdrop. Photograph it under consistent, even lighting with no flash. Take the photograph from a fixed distance (30 cm works well). This image is what you will upload to derive the key. Store it safely — it is the only way to regenerate the key without the physical sphere.

The same sphere photographed consistently will always produce the same key. Minor angle and lighting differences are normalised. Major differences (different sphere, very dark image) will produce a different key.
