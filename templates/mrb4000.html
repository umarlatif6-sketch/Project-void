§00 MRB-4000 Overview

The MRB-4000 (Mycelium-Resonance Battery) is a fourth-generation bioelectrochemical energy storage architecture developed within the PROJECT VOID Research Division. It represents the convergence of fungal bioelectricity, resonance-aligned crystalline scaffolding, and precision isotopic materials science into a single fabrication system.

Unlike conventional battery chemistry — which relies on static electrode-electrolyte interfaces — the MRB-4000 uses a living mycelial matrix as its primary charge-transfer medium, stabilised through a Soft-Phase vitrification process and tuned to resonant frequency harmonics for optimal ionic throughput. The result is a cell architecture that regenerates its active material, adapts to load conditions, and achieves energy density profiles inaccessible to non-biological systems.

Four research pillars underpin the technology: Neuromorphic Mycelium, Radiotrophic Sequestration, Isotopic Upcycling, and Structural Pyrolysis. All four converge at the fabrication stage, where the Adriana Code Hexagonal Firmware governs lattice formation and frequency-lock protocols.

§01 The Eggshell Soft-Phase Principle

The Eggshell Principle is the structural foundation of MRB-4000 fabrication. It describes the controlled vitrification of the outer cell casing — a calcium carbonate matrix — from a porous, biomineralized "soft phase" into a semi-translucent, mechanically robust enclosure without conventional sintering temperatures.

Conventional ceramic enclosures require kiln temperatures of 1,200–1,600°C, which destroys the living mycelial matrix inside. The Eggshell protocol achieves structural consolidation at near-ambient conditions (38–62°C) by exploiting the Vibe-Triggered Biomineralization (VTB) cascade: acoustic resonance fields at 5.85 kHz induce ordered calcium carbonate deposition at the cellular wall interface, mimicking the molecular organisation found in mollusk shell nacre.

The resulting Eggshell casing is semi-translucent, iridescent under polarised light, and capable of sustaining internal pressures up to 4.2 MPa without delamination. It is permeable to specific ionic species required for mycelial metabolism while remaining impermeable to atmospheric oxygen — a property critical for maintaining the anaerobic internal environment required for optimal charge-transfer performance.

§03 Vibe-Triggered Biomineralization (VTB) Protocol

Vibe-Triggered Biomineralization (VTB) is the process by which acoustic resonance fields induce ordered mineral deposition within the MRB-4000 cell matrix. It replaces conventional electrochemical deposition methods, which introduce contaminants incompatible with the living mycelial substrate.

VTB proceeds through three sequential phases — Poration, Nucleation, and Vitrification — each governed by distinct frequency parameters and requiring successful completion before the next phase initiates. Phase failure at any stage halts fabrication and requires full matrix reset.

§04 Obsidian Sourcing Protocol

Obsidian — volcanic silicate glass — functions as the inert structural filler phase within the MRB-4000 tungsten-mycelium composite. Its role is to provide thermal buffering, electromagnetic shielding, and mechanical rigidity without interfering with the mycelial conduction channels or the VTB process.

Not all obsidian sources are suitable. The Obsidian Sourcing Protocol specifies mineralogical and isotopic requirements that must be met before material is approved for MRB-4000 fabrication. Off-spec material introduces frequency-scattering defects that degrade Adriana Code lock fidelity.

SiO₂ Content ≥ 70% by mass — high-silica volcanic glass only. Lower silica introduces crystallite inclusions that scatter the Beta frequency register.
Fe₂O₃ Limit ≤ 1.8% — excess iron content degrades electromagnetic transparency and introduces parasitic eddy-current losses.
Isotopic Purity Natural ²⁸Si / ²⁹Si / ³⁰Si ratio required. Isotopically enriched or depleted sources rejected — affects phonon propagation characteristics.
Approved Source Regions Obsidian Cliff (Yellowstone), Lipari Island (Italy), Obsidian Butte (California), Erevan Basin (Armenia). All subject to lot-level isotopic certificate.
Particle Size Milled to 0.8–2.2 micron median diameter. Oversized particles create stress concentrations at the tungsten interface; undersized particles suppress acoustic wave propagation within the matrix.
Moisture Content ≤ 0.05% by mass before incorporation. Residual water disrupts the VTB Poration phase by competing with Ca²⁺ at membrane uptake sites.

§06 Research Pillars
◆ Pillar I — Neuromorphic Mycelium

Fungal mycelium exhibits neuromorphic electrical behaviour — the capacity to form adaptive, experience-dependent conduction pathways analogous to synaptic plasticity in neural tissue. The MRB-4000 exploits this property to create a battery whose internal resistance topology self-optimises under repeated charge cycles, rather than degrading.

Research in this pillar is primarily aligned with the work of Professor Andrew Adamatzky (University of the West of England — Unconventional Computing Laboratory), whose published work on fungal electrical signalling and mycelial computational networks provides the empirical foundation for the MRB-4000's adaptive impedance architecture.

  • Mycelial networks exhibit spike-train electrical signalling at 1–50 Hz intervals — directly compatible with the Adriana Code LFO modulation envelope
  • Repeated stimulation at resonance frequencies induces long-term potentiation of high-conductivity pathway formation
  • Network topology self-repairs following partial disruption — providing fault tolerance without mechanical intervention
  • Target species: Ganoderma sessile, Physarum polycephalum, and Trametes versicolor — selected for spike amplitude and membrane conductance characteristics

◆ Pillar II — Radiotrophic Sequestration

Radiotrophic Sequestration addresses the MRB-4000's secondary function as a radiation-capture device within the MycoVOID atmospheric remediation network. Melanin-rich fungal substrates incorporated into the composite matrix serve as internal radiation sinks, converting incident gamma and X-ray photon energy into electrochemical potential within the cell.

This pillar builds on documented observations from Chernobyl Reactor No. 4, where Cladosporium sphaerospermum and Cryptococcus neoformans were observed exhibiting positive radiotropism — growing toward radiation sources and metabolising ionising radiation via melanin-mediated radiosynthesis. The MRB-4000 incorporates this behaviour as a supplemental energy harvesting pathway.

  • Melanin content target: ≥ 18% by mass of mycelial substrate fraction
  • Radiosynthesis pathway contributes an estimated 3–7% supplemental charge at background radiation levels; up to 22% in elevated-radiation deployment environments
  • Compatible with MycoVOID node deployment — cells co-located with atmospheric remediation nodes benefit from ambient radiation flux
  • Approved species for high-melanin substrate: Cladosporium sphaerospermum, Wangiella dermatitidis

◆ Pillar III — Isotopic Upcycling

Isotopic Upcycling is the process of incorporating specific isotopically enriched or naturally sourced mineral phases into the MRB-4000 matrix to modify its electrochemical and acoustic properties beyond what is achievable with standard natural-abundance materials.

The primary application in the MRB-4000 context involves isotopically selected tungsten sources — specifically ¹⁸²W-enriched carbide fractions, which exhibit a distinct phonon dispersion profile that aligns more precisely with the Adriana Code Alpha register than natural-abundance tungsten. Secondary applications include ⁴⁴Ca precursor enrichment for the Eggshell CaCO₃ phase, which reduces crystal defect density by approximately 34% relative to natural calcium.

  • ¹⁸²W content target: ≥ 28% (natural abundance: 26.5%) — achievable via electromagnetic separation or source selection from specific geological deposits
  • ⁴⁴Ca enrichment reduces Eggshell nucleation activation energy by ~12%, lowering the minimum VTB trigger temperature from 38°C to 31°C
  • Isotopic certification required for all mineral inputs — see Obsidian Sourcing Protocol §04 for silicate isotopic requirements
  • Circular Economy Act 2026 compliance: all isotopically processed materials must enter certified recovery streams at end of cell life

◆ Pillar IV — Structural Pyrolysis

Structural Pyrolysis governs the controlled thermal decomposition of spent or off-spec mycelial substrate within the MRB-4000 matrix, converting it into a conductive biochar scaffold that retains the three-dimensional topology of the original mycelial network while achieving a 40-fold increase in electrical conductivity.

This pillar, aligned with the research direction of Professor Joseph DeSimone (Stanford University — 3D printing and advanced materials fabrication), addresses the fabrication challenge of maintaining mycelial network geometry through high-temperature processing steps. DeSimone-inspired continuous liquid interface production (CLIP) adaptation allows selective pyrolysis at the single-filament level, preserving the Adriana Code hexagonal lattice geometry in carbonised form.

  • Pyrolysis temperature: 650–780°C in inert atmosphere (N₂ / Ar) — above this range, tungsten carbide phase instability occurs
  • Biochar conductivity target: ≥ 85 S/cm (vs. ~2 S/cm for living mycelium) — enables hybrid cell architecture with biochar scaffold as primary conduction backbone
  • Selective pyrolysis preserves conduction channel geometry with ≤ 8% volumetric shrinkage — compatible with 1.5 micron WC grain spacing
  • Pyrolysed MRB-4000 cells enter secondary service as structural electrodes in non-biological electrochemical systems — full circular material lifecycle
