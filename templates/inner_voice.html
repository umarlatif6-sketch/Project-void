§00 Concept Overview

The Inner Voice Module is a VOID research concept that reads neuromuscular signals generated during internal speech — sub-vocalisations that occur when a person thinks in words — without producing any audible sound. When a person forms words silently, the jaw, tongue, and throat muscles fire in micro-contractions detectable by surface electromyography (sEMG). These signals carry the full phonetic signature of intended speech.

This maps directly onto the architecture pioneered by MIT's AlterEgo project. Where AlterEgo transmits captured signals to cloud inference servers, VOID's Rumble-and-Decode approach processes everything locally on the MRB-4000 — the internal monologue never leaves the body. Intent decoding is handled by the Adriana SDK running on the ESP32-S3 with BioSig 3.9, with decoded intent delivered to applications via the MRB-4000 VTB Protocol trigger.

The result is a silent speech interface that is sovereign by architecture: no microphone, no internet uplink, no proprietary inference cloud. The biological signal, the computation, and the output all remain within a single closed-loop system that the user controls.

VOID Architecture Principle

"The internal monologue is the most intimate signal a human generates. It must never leave the body unencrypted, and it must never be processed by a system the speaker does not own. Rumble-and-Decode is the architecture that enforces this. Local inference or no inference."

§03 VOID-Mask — Physical Design

The VOID-Mask is the wearable housing for the Inner Voice electrode array. Its design goal is stealth: it must look like an ordinary earmuff or audio headset, with no visible medical-device aesthetics. The electrode contact points are positioned on the interior surface, making contact with the jaw and behind-ear anatomy when worn.

The shell is moulded from Shore 85A thermoplastic polyurethane (TPU) — the same material used in industrial protective earmuffs. It is flexible enough to conform to varying face geometries and firm enough to maintain electrode pressure contact without adhesive.

Conductive carbon-black channels are screen-printed on the mask interior using silver conductive ink. These replace conventional wiring, creating flush internal signal traces that are invisible from outside the device and protected from flexion fatigue by the elastomeric substrate.

Electrode Point A
Left Masseter
Primary articulation channel. Masseter fires during jaw movements associated with phoneme formation. Left channel provides dominant signal for bilabial and velar consonants.
Electrode Point B
Right Masseter
Bilateral symmetry reference. Differencing left and right Masseter channels cancels shared-mode artefacts (chewing, swallowing) and isolates lateralised speech motor signals.
Electrode Point C
Suprahyoid (Sublingual)
Tongue and palate motion correlates. The suprahyoid group activates during vowel formation and alveolar consonants. Positioned beneath the chin, flush with the underside of the jaw.
Electrode Point D
Mastoid Reference
Ground and noise reference electrode. Placed on the mastoid process (behind the ear) — a bony, electrically quiet surface. Used to drive the right-leg drive (RLD) circuit for common-mode rejection.

Form factor: Stealth earmuff profile — 85 × 65 mm per cup, 8 mm interior depth, 22 mm headband arc. Total device weight including battery: ~95 g. The ADS1299 breakout and ESP32-S3 board are housed in the left cup behind the driver plate, connected to the right cup electrodes via the flexible headband internal cable channel.

§04 Centralised Mind-Reading vs Sovereign Processing

The core architectural divide in silent speech technology is not technical — it is political. The question is not whether sEMG can decode sub-vocalisation (it can). The question is where the decoding occurs and who controls the inference model.

▲ Their Model — Centralised
▲ Raw sEMG waveforms transmitted to cloud inference servers. The complete phonetic signature of your internal speech leaves your body in plaintext or lightly encrypted form.
▲ Inference model is proprietary and opaque. The company defines what counts as a "word" and what gets logged, retained, or monetised.
♺ Terms of Service grant broad rights to "improve the product" using biometric data — including sub-vocal speech patterns — without per-use consent.
♺ Cloud dependency means the device fails without internet. Functionality can be revoked remotely. Updates to the model are pushed without user review.
♺ No hardware access: the device is a locked peripheral. Users cannot inspect, modify, or audit the inference pipeline.
◆ VOID Model — Sovereign
◆ All inference runs on the MRB-4000 host device. sEMG signals never leave the local BLE link. No uplink required at any stage from electrode to application output.
◆ Adriana SDK classifier is trained per user, on-device. The user's personal phoneme model is stored locally in encrypted flash. No cloud training corpus required.
◆ Open BioSig processing pipeline. Every filter, every threshold, every feature extraction step is inspectable and modifiable by the user or their delegate.
◆ Offline-first by design. The system operates identically with or without network access. Functionality cannot be remotely revoked or metered.
◆ Full hardware access. Schematics, firmware, and BioSig configuration are user-owned. The inference model weights belong to the person who generated the training data.
