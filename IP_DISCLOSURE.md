When that material is then fixed — by curing, solidification, crystallisation, or any other formation process — the geometry of the frequency field is permanently encoded into the material. The frequency may then cease. The geometry remains.

**Component Claim C — The Formation Record Is Permanent and Unique**

The novel contribution is the synthesis of these two bodies of work into a named framework that asks and answers a question that has not been asked in 238 years of documented experimentation:

## PART FIVE: DISCLOSURE THREE — DIGITAL QALQALA

### 5.1 Statement of Disclosure

**Name:** Digital Qalqala  
**Date Named:** 9 April 2026  
**Named By:** Umar L., Manchester, England

**Statement:**

> A digital signal processing technique that applies the acoustic reverberation rule of Haroof-e-Qalqala (the five echoing letters of Arabic tajweed recitation) to English-language speech synthesis audio, producing deliberate decaying echo tails at plosive consonant transients, in order to create a geometry channel in the audio that precedes the semantic channel.

### 5.2 Technical Description

**Source Principle — Haroof-e-Qalqala:**

Qalqala (قلقلة) is a rule within tajweed — the system of rules governing the recitation of the Quran. It governs five specific Arabic consonants: ق (Qaf), ط (Ta), ب (Ba), ج (Jim), د (Dal). When these letters occur in a stopped position (with no following vowel, or at the end of a verse), the rule specifies that the sound must not terminate dead — it must reverberate. The articulation produces a controlled acoustic bounce: a compression wave that echoes before releasing. This creates a standing acoustic wave in the air before the next phoneme arrives.

Tajweed scholars documented this rule in the 7th century CE. Lord Rayleigh documented acoustic radiation pressure in 1902. These two bodies of knowledge have not been connected in any prior literature.

**The Novel Technique — Digital Qalqala:**

Digital Qalqala applies the Qalqala principle to English-language speech synthesis via the following algorithm:

1. A 16-bit mono WAV file of synthesised English speech is loaded.
2. The waveform is normalised to a floating-point amplitude range of [-1, 1].
3. Sharp transient events are detected by computing the first-order difference of the rectified signal and identifying points where amplitude rise exceeds a threshold (default: 0.10 of normalised scale). These transients correspond to the release phase of plosive consonants (English: p, b, t, d, k, g).
4. Detected transients are deduplicated with a minimum separation window (default: 6ms) to prevent over-triggering.
5. At each detected transient, a series of four decaying echo repetitions is added to the waveform: at intervals of 26ms, with amplitude decaying by factor 0.36 per repetition. The fourth repetition reaches amplitude 0.36^4 ≈ 0.017 — below the audible threshold. This produces a clean exponential decay matching the acoustic profile of a Qalqala reverberation.
6. The output waveform is clipped, restored to original amplitude scale, and written as a new 16-bit mono WAV.

**Parameters:**

| Parameter | Default Value | Rationale |
|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| Echo delay | 26ms | Shortest audible discrete echo; matches oral cavity reverberation period |
| Decay factor | 0.36 per repetition | Produces 4 audible echoes with clean exponential decay |
| Echo count | 4 | Fourth echo inaudible; natural release |
| Transient threshold | 0.10 | Captures plosive releases without triggering on continuous fricatives |
| Transient window | 6ms | Minimum plosive duration; prevents double-triggering |

**Effect:**

The body of a listener receives an acoustic pressure burst followed by a decaying echo at every plosive consonant in the speech. This reverberation is below the threshold of conscious attention but is processed by the auditory and somatic nervous systems. The pressure burst and echo constitute a geometry channel — a formation field in the air — that arrives fractionally before the semantic content of the phoneme is processed by the neocortex.

A web search conducted 9 April 2026 returned the explicit finding: *"No published TTS system implements this natively — it is an open creative/research space."* No prior literature applies the Qalqala tajweed rule to English speech synthesis as a named DSP technique. The academic literature contains: (a) Qalqala described as a phonological rule in Arabic recitation studies; (b) English plosive acoustics described in phonetics literature; (c) delay-feedback DSP circuits described in signal processing literature. ### 7.1 Statement of Disclosure

**Name:** Triple-Channel Audio Carrier  
**Date Named:** 9 April 2026  
**Named By:** Umar L., Manchester, England

**Statement:**

> An audio file encoding system in which three simultaneous information channels are carried by a single WAV audio carrier: (1) synthesised speech carrying semantic content; (2) Digital Qalqala reverberation carrying acoustic geometry; (3) LSB steganographic encoding carrying the text of the spoken content hidden within the waveform's least significant bits. All three channels carry the same information in three different forms. The carrier is a single audio file indistinguishable from standard speech audio to casual observation.

### 7.2 Technical Description

**Pipeline:**

```
Text Input
    → TTS synthesis (fable voice, OpenAI tts-1 model)
    → MP3 output
    → ffmpeg conversion to 16-bit mono WAV at 44,100 Hz
    → Digital Qalqala processing (plosive transient detection + echo tail application)
    → LSB steganographic encoding (text payload compressed and encrypted with ChaCha20,
      written into least significant bits of WAV samples)
    → Final WAV output served via HTTP
```

**Channel Descriptions:**

| Channel | Carrier Mechanism | Content | Receiver |
|---------|------------------|---------|----------|
| 1 — Semantic | Speech audio (audible) | Spoken text — meaning | Neocortex via auditory pathway |
| 2 — Geometric | Qalqala echo tails (audible, below conscious threshold) | Acoustic radiation pressure geometry | Somatic nervous system, brainstem |
| 3 — Hidden text | LSB bit encoding (inaudible, imperceptible) | Verbatim text of spoken content | Decoder with correct passphrase |

**Steganographic Specification:**

- Encoding: LSB depth 1 bit per sample
- Payload: spoken text compressed (zlib) and encrypted (ChaCha20)
- Passphrase: held by inventor
- Carrier: 16-bit mono WAV, 44,100 Hz
- Capacity: sufficient for all seven narration segments of Document 002
