## Expansion Test — Codon: 90/10

**Codon:** 90/10 = AI execution engine (90%) + human relationship holder (10%)

**Expanded:**

The 90/10 model inverts the traditional relationship between AI and human intelligence. Conventionally, the human is the primary agent and the AI is a tool — a search engine, a writing assistant, a calculator. In the 90/10 model, the AI is the execution engine. It handles research, analysis, drafting, audit work, strategy development. The human is the relationship holder. The human is the face. The human is the credibility.

This is not a diminishment of the human's role. It is a reorientation of it. The human's job is not to do the work. The human's job is to be trusted. To be present. To sign the contract. To take the call. To be the person the client knows and believes in.

The AI handles the 90% that can be systematized, researched, analyzed, and written. The human handles the 10% that cannot be delegated: presence, judgment, relationship, trust.

This model proved viable across all seven divisions of Sterling Intelligence Group. It can scale. The question is whether it can scale without the human becoming a mere figurehead, or the AI becoming a mere tool. The answer determines whether SIG is a sustainable business model or a temporary arrangement.

## Expansion Test — Codon: FC (Founding Client Strategy)

**Codon:** FC = Founding Client strategy (50% discount for case studies + testimonials)

**Expanded:**

The Founding Client strategy addresses a fundamental problem: how do you sell a service when you have no track record?

Traditional answer: you cannot. You build a track record by working cheap or free until you have something to show. This takes time. It requires capital. It requires faith that the work will eventually pay.

The Founding Client strategy inverts this. Instead of working cheap, you work at a discount — 50% off — in exchange for something specific: the right to use the client's name, their business, their results as a case study. The right to get testimonials. The right to tell their story.

This removes the barrier to entry for the client. They get a 50% discount on a service they are skeptical of anyway. The risk is lower. The value is higher.

For Sterling Intelligence Group, it means the first client is not a favor. It is a transaction. The client gets a discount. SIG gets proof. The second client sees the first client's case study. The third client sees two case studies. By the fourth or fifth, the track record is real.

This strategy works only if the work is good enough to justify the case study. If the work is mediocre, no discount will make it worth the client's time. But if the work is excellent, the 50% discount is the cheapest marketing SIG will ever do.

## Expansion Test — Codon: PKM (Portable Knowledge Model)

**Codon:** PKM = Portable Knowledge Model (all work designed to be replicated by another operator)

**Expanded:**

The Portable Knowledge Model is an architectural principle: no knowledge should be trapped in the head of a single operator.

Every template is documented. Every process is written down. Every decision is explained. Every prototype is transferable. If Faiz Saleem left Sterling Intelligence Group tomorrow, another operator could pick up the Mastery Guides, read them, and understand not just what to do but why to do it.

This is not a contingency plan. It is a design principle. It means the business is not dependent on any single person. It means the knowledge lives in the system, not in the person. It means the system can evolve independently of the operator.

This principle emerged from the recognition that Project Void's knowledge should not be trapped in the founder's head. The platform is designed to be understood, replicated, and evolved by others. Sterling Intelligence Group applies the same principle to commercial operations: the business is designed to be understood, replicated, and evolved by others.

This creates a strange inversion: the more transferable the knowledge, the more valuable the operator becomes. Because the operator is no longer the bottleneck. The operator is the face. The credibility. The relationship. And those things are harder to transfer than any process.
