# AL-JABR 286 REPAIR PROTOCOL

**Codon Name:** [Al-Jabr-286-Repair-Law]
**Logic Source:** Al-Jabr as the reunion of broken parts
**Application:** Code, docs, routes, world-state, memory rails, and protocol continuity

## 1. Governing Law

Al-Jabr 286 is not a discard law. It is a repair law.

Its function is not to pretend broken pieces were never broken. Its function is to identify which pieces still carry true structure, remove corruption and false joins, and recompose a valid whole that can stand under proof.

In Project VOID terms:
- Broken is not the same as useless.
- Damaged is not the same as sovereign.
- Repair is not the same as blind restoration.

The protocol repairs by verification, not sentiment.

## 2. The Four Repair States

Every broken piece discovered in the Void must be placed into one of four states:

1. **Recoverable**
   - The piece still carries valid structure.
   - It can be repaired or recomposed without violating the platform's governing laws.

2. **Quarantined**
   - The piece may still contain value, but its current state cannot be trusted.
   - It must be isolated until proof or reconstruction is possible.

3. **Superseded**
   - The piece is coherent, but a stronger form already exists.
   - It should remain in the record and not be reintroduced as the active form by mistake.

4. **False Join**
   - The piece appears to fit, but introduces drift, contradiction, corruption, or unverifiable claims.
   - It must not be recomposed into the active body.

## 3. The 286 Repair Sequence

When a damaged area is found, execute the sequence in order:

1. **Witness**
   - Name the break precisely.
   - Do not speak about "mess" in the abstract. Identify the exact seam, drift, contradiction, or missing rail.

2. **Sort**
   - Separate recoverable structure from corruption, duplication, and noise.
   - Do not glue intact and corrupted pieces together just because they are adjacent.

3. **Recompose**
   - Rebuild the valid pattern from verified parts.
   - Preserve origin, naming law, and continuity wherever those are still true.

4. **Verify**
   - Check the recomposed form against the platform's proof surfaces: tests, protocol law, route behavior, naming discipline, and Chronicle continuity.

5. **Seal**
   - Record what was repaired, what was quarantined, what was superseded, and what remains open.
   - A repair that is not written down becomes a future fracture.

## 4. What Must Be Preserved

The protocol is fail-closed about these elements:
- Origin and naming law
- Al-Jabr identity and verification surfaces
- Chronicle continuity
- Security and authorization boundaries
- Proven operator rails

If a repair breaks one of these to make the surface look cleaner, it is not repair. It is aesthetic damage.

## 5. Practical Triage Targets

This repair doctrine applies to:
- Broken documentation that lost the system's true meaning
- Drifted codex entries and protocol summaries
- Damaged route behavior or mismatched permissions
- Partial world-state or incomplete creator workflows
- Historical artifacts that must be retained but not reactivated
- Scars and open risks that need quarantine instead of denial

## 6. Agent Rule

Future agents must not default to either extreme:
- not "keep every broken piece as-is"
- not "throw damaged pieces away"

The correct action is:
**recover what is true, quarantine what is unsafe, leave superseded forms in the record, and recompose the living body under proof.**

## 7. Operational Question

Before any repair closes, ask:

**Did we restore the sovereign pattern, or did we only hide the fracture?**

If the fracture is only hidden, the repair is incomplete.
