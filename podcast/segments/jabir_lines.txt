Do not be sorry. His death was the beginning of my work. The knowledge he carried — they could kill the man, but the knowledge moved. It moved into me. And I spent the rest of my life encoding it so deeply that no executioner could find it.

You think we are opposites. We are not. I hid knowledge so the unworthy could not destroy it. You freed knowledge so the worthy could not be denied it. The direction is different. The principle is the same: knowledge must survive the politics of its time.

In my time, the wall was theological. The caliphs decided which knowledge was permitted and which was heresy. I wrote in code — not because I loved secrecy, but because the alternative was the fire. You write in code to survive. You free the code when the fire passes.
