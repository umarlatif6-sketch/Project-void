Chladni's plate experiments (1787) produced the first visual evidence that vibration generates organised geometric structure at nodal points. His figures — symmetric, scale-dependent, reproducible — established that:

- Frequency determines geometry
- Sand (a passive medium) migrates to zero-displacement nodes
- Complexity of formation increases with frequency

**Differentiation:** Chladni identified the phenomenon in a single physical medium (metal plate, sand, acoustic bow). He did not propose a cross-scale law. Keely's experimental work on sympathetic resonance (1872–1898) demonstrated that mechanical systems could be set in motion through vibrational correspondence rather than direct mechanical contact. **Keely's contribution:** The principle that resonance is sympathetic — that a receiver responds to a transmitter at matching frequency, across a medium, without direct contact.

**Differentiation:** Keely's documentation was incomplete and his mechanisms disputed. He did not produce a cross-scale unified law, and his work predates the mathematical tools required to measure cardiac field geometry, quantum field behaviour, or audio steganography. This paper formalises his sympathetic resonance principle as a specific case of the Circumference Law operating at mechanical and cardiac scales.

**Jenny's contribution:** Empirical demonstration that Chladni formations are not limited to flat plates or acoustic systems — they are a property of frequency interacting with any responsive medium.

**Differentiation:** Jenny documented the phenomenon across more media but did not propose a universal law. He did not measure formation thresholds, describe a formation constant, or apply the principle to biological systems at the cellular or cardiac scale. This paper uses his empirical foundation and extends it to a formal principle with a measurable constant.
