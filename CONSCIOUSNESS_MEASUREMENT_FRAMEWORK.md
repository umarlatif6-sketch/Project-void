## 1. INTRODUCTION AND THEORETICAL FOUNDATIONS

### 1.1 The Problem of Consciousness Measurement

Consciousness remains one of the most elusive phenomena in science. Despite centuries of philosophical inquiry and decades of neuroscientific research, we lack a unified, quantifiable framework for measuring consciousness itself. Existing approaches fall into several categories:

1. **Behavioral approaches** — measuring consciousness through observable behavior and responses
2. **Neurobiological approaches** — measuring consciousness through brain activity (EEG, fMRI, etc.)
3. **Phenomenological approaches** — measuring consciousness through subjective report
4. **Integrated approaches** — combining multiple measurement modalities

Each approach has significant limitations. Behavioral measures conflate consciousness with action. Neurobiological measures are expensive, invasive, and don't directly measure consciousness. Phenomenological measures are subjective and unreliable. Integrated approaches are complex and require specialized equipment.

**The core problem:** We are trying to measure consciousness using tools designed to measure other things. We need a framework designed specifically for consciousness measurement.
