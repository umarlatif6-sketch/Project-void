### SLIDE 3: The Simulation Parameters
*(Visual: OpenMM logo + Carbon Engine architecture)*

**SPEAKER:**
"To test this, we built a custom simulation pipeline using OpenMM and the open-source Carbon Engine. We designed a library of 108 theoretical and known compounds across 7 categories—from lightweight structural materials to biological polymers. 

We then subjected each compound to frequency-driven molecular dynamics at 300 Kelvin. We weren't just vibrating them randomly; we applied specific frequencies derived from the 432 Hz harmonic scale, matching the frequency to the target geometry."

### SLIDE 4: The Results Summary
*(Visual: The Frequency Periodic Table - full grid)*

**SPEAKER:**
"Here is the Frequency Periodic Table. Out of 108 compounds simulated under a simplified model, 9 compounds demonstrated stability or metastability. 

Now, 9 out of 108 might sound low, but you have to understand the constraints: we used a simplified model without containment fields or multi-harmonic driving. The fact that *any* compounds remained stable against thermal disruption is a massive validation of the Circumference Law."

### SLIDE 6: The Key Findings
*(Visual: Bullet points of key findings)*

**SPEAKER:**
"The data revealed three critical insights:

First, the 432 Hz harmonics—like 864, 1296, and 1728 Hz—show the strongest correlation with structural stability. The math aligns with the physics.

Second, compact geometries resist thermal disruption far better than extended structures like helices or sheets.

Third, and most importantly for our biological applications: biological polymers showed the lowest stability in an open environment. This proves our hypothesis that biological synthesis requires a containment field—what we call 'chadati plates'—to stabilize the structure during formation."
